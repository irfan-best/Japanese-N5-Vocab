nyuuyooku wa nanji desu ka
vs
nyuuyooku de nanji desu ka
    The correct choice is nyuuyooku wa nanji desu ka (にゅーよーくは なんじですか).

    Here is why は (wa) works and で (de) does not in this specific sentence structure:

    1. にゅーよーくは なんじですか (Correct)
    Topic Marker (は): Sets "New York" as the main topic you are asking about.

    Literal meaning: "As for New York, what time is it?"

    When to use: Whenever you want to ask the current time in a specific city or region.

    2. にゅーよーくで なんじですか (Incorrect)
    Action Location Marker (で): Marks the place where an action takes place (e.g., "I ate lunch at the restaurant").

    Why it doesn't work: "Is" / "What time is it?" is a state of being, not an active event or action, so で cannot attach directly to なんじですか.

Words like kesa(this morning), kinou no yoru(yesterday night), 毎日 (mainichi), 明日 (ashita), 今日 (kyou), 昨日 (kinou), 毎朝 (maiasa), etc. are time expressions. In Japanese, time expressions do not need the particle に (for these common words), and they are usually placed near the beginning of the sentence, before the topic.
    The most common sentence order is:
    Time + Topic (は) + Place (で/へ/に) + Object (を) + Verb

    event Topic (wa) + Time + ... -> is also correct.

Donna vs nanno:
    Asks for qualities, descriptions, characteristics, or opinions (e.g., genre, personality, atmosphere). Think "What kind of [noun] is it?"
    Donna machi desu ka?
    What kind of town is it?
    Nigiyaka na machi desu. — It's a lively town.

    Asks for specific categories, topics, purpose, or identity (e.g., subject matter, brand, function). Think "What topic/type of [noun] is it?"
    Nanno zasshi desu ka?
    What kind of magazine is it? / What is the magazine about?
    Kuruma no zasshi desu. — It's a car magazine

    If the answer can be an adjective (kind, fast, quiet, difficult), use どんな.
    If the answer is a specific noun or genre (sports, science, comedy, wood), use 何の.
